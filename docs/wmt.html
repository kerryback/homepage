Fiscal 2026 ended 2026-01-31 is year 0. All figures in USD millions.

15 judgment calls made without review. Each is flagged where it was made; open to see them all.
  • Line items Operating lease right-of-use assets. Excluded with the operating lease liability, because rent of about $2.4 billion a year is inside OSG&A and so already inside EBITDA. Treating the liability as debt instead would subtract about $15.6 billion (roughly $1.94 a share) and double-count rent.
  • Line items Finance lease right-of-use assets, net. Excluded, with finance lease obligations treated as debt. The alternative is to fold these $6.1 billion of assets into net PP&E, which would lower the turnover and the depreciation rate together; future finance-leased assets are funded through the capex plug either way.
  • Line items Other long-term assets. Excluded as one line. About $5.7 billion of it is equity investments and the Asda debt investment, which are non-operating; classifying the whole line as non-operating would add $14.1 billion, and carving out just the investments would add about $5.7 billion (roughly $0.71 a share).

Driver by driver

Sales growth

History 2023 6.7%, 2024 6.0%, 2025 5.1%, 2026 4.7%  ·  Suggested 4.5%, 4.3%, 4.0%, 4.0%, 3.8%, then 3.5% forever  ·  Moving it 1pp each way moves the value 25.4%

Sales growth is total revenues (net sales plus membership and other income) year over year. Walmart guides one year at a time in constant currency, so year 1 should sit inside the current guidance range and years 2 to 5 are a fade toward a perpetual nominal rate. What moves it: U.S. comps (transactions and ticket), eCommerce and marketplace growth, international (China, India via Flipkart, Mexico), food inflation, and pharmacy pricing (maximum fair pricing on GLP-1 and other drugs). History is 6.7, 6.0, 5.1 and 4.7 percent for FY2023 to FY2026, a decelerating series partly flattered by inflation in FY2023 and FY2024. A higher path would need eCommerce and advertising to keep compounding at 20-plus percent on a larger base; a lower path would follow from a weaker consumer or a strong dollar, since the guide is constant currency and the model is in reported dollars.

“Full year constant currency sales are expected to grow between 3.54.5%.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2026Q4_earnings_call_2026-02-19.txt, guidance
“We're raising our fiscal year sales guidance to 4% to 5% from 3.5% to 4.5% previously.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, guidance
“to have what is roughly $0.75 trillion of a revenue base and be able to grow at 4%, 5%, 6% a year, that's meaningful growth.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, Q&A
“Our total revenues increased $32.2 billion or 4.7% and $32.9 billion or 5.1% for fiscal 2026 and 2025, respectively, when compared to the previous fiscal year.”10-K, 10k/WMT_10-K_FY2026_filed2026-03-13.htm, Item 7 MD&A

Why the suggestion sits where it does. Year 1 at 4.5 percent is the midpoint of the raised FY2027 guide of 4 to 5 percent. It fades by about a quarter point a year to 3.75 percent in year 5, then 3.5 percent forever, close to the bottom of the 4 to 6 percent range the CFO describes, because a $750 billion base cannot outgrow nominal GDP for ever.

EBITDA margin

History 2022 6.4%, 2023 5.1%, 2024 6.0%, 2025 6.2%, 2026 6.2%  ·  Suggested 6.5%, 6.7%, 6.8%, 6.9%, 7.0%, then 7.0% forever  ·  Moving it 1pp each way moves the value 64.4%

Judgment call Margin expands from 6.17 to 7.0 percent, above every year of history (5.1 to 6.4 percent), on management's claim that operating income grows well faster than sales. Holding the FY2026 margin flat is the alternative a skeptic would argue; it is the largest single judgment in this valuation.

The statements report no EBITDA line, so EBITDA here is operating income plus depreciation and amortization from the cash flow statement. It is after rent on operating leases (inside OSG&A) and after stock compensation, and before finance lease amortization, which sits in D&A. What moves it: business mix (advertising, membership and marketplace carry far higher margins than retail), eCommerce incremental margins, automation savings, price investment, self-insurance claims, fuel and tariff costs, and LIFO. History is 6.4, 5.1, 6.0, 6.2 and 6.2 percent for FY2022 to FY2026; FY2023 was depressed by opioid legal charges and inventory markdowns. FY2026 carries roughly $0.9 billion of extra self-insurance claims and a $0.7 billion PhonePe share-based compensation charge, about 0.2 percent of sales together. Because D&A is growing faster than sales, EBITDA margin has to rise just to hold EBIT margin flat, so a flat EBITDA margin is itself a mildly bearish call. A lower path is right if price investment and tariff refunds go back to customers permanently.

“Operating income guidance reflects a higher level of confidence relative to prior year's original guidance that we can deliver growth in the upper half of the range depicted in our framework.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2026Q4_earnings_call_2026-02-19.txt, guidance
“We're assuming continued margin expansion driven by favorable business mix, automation benefits and productivity, and less headwinds from merchandise category mix.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2026Q4_earnings_call_2026-02-19.txt, guidance
“On operating income for 2027 and the outer years, look, we have a lot of confidence in our ability to continue to grow the bottom line at a much faster rate than we have the top line.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, Q&A
“If you look at the profit drivers of our business, almost half of the growth came from areas like membership, advertising, marketplace.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, Q&A
“The increase for fiscal 2026 was primarily due to higher self-insured general liability claims expense in the U.S. of approximately $0.9 billion, influenced by rising costs to resolve claims across retail and related industries, a charge of $0.7 billion related to modification of certain share-based compensation arrangements for our PhonePe subsidiary and increased depreciation related to our capital investments.”10-K, 10k/WMT_10-K_FY2026_filed2026-03-13.htm, Item 7 MD&A
“We now expect more than $2 billion of incremental fuel-related costs this year above and beyond our original guidance assumptions”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, guidance

Why the suggestion sits where it does. Year 1 at 6.45 percent gives operating income growth of roughly 8 percent on 4.5 percent sales growth, the top of the original 6 to 8 percent guide and consistent with the raise in August. Expansion then slows to about 10 to 15 basis points a year, ending at 7.0 percent, which puts terminal EBIT margin near 4.6 percent against 4.2 percent in FY2026.

Next-year sales / net PP&E

History 2023 6.43×, 2024 6.15×, 2025 5.94×  ·  Suggested 5.20×, 5.00×, 5.00×, 5.00×, 5.00×, then 5.00× forever  ·  Moving it 5% of itself each way moves the value 15.2%

Judgment call Turnover held at 5.0, below the 5.9 to 6.4 of FY2023 to FY2025, on the view that the automation build-out is a permanent step up in capital intensity. If turnover recovered to 6.0 once capex peaks, terminal capex would fall from about 3.0 to about 2.5 percent of sales and value would rise materially.

Turnover is next year's sales over this year's net PP&E. It has fallen every year: 6.43, 6.15 and 5.94 for FY2023 to FY2025, and net PP&E grew 13 percent in FY2026 against 4.7 percent sales growth, so FY2026's turnover on guided FY2027 sales is about 5.5. Walmart is spending on supply chain automation, store remodels, fulfillment and technology, and says spending peaks this year and next. Because capex is the plug, this driver sets capex: steady-state capex is (g + d) over the turnover, as a share of sales. Use the capex guidance (about 4 percent of sales in FY2027) to calibrate year 1, then decide whether the asset base stays more capital-intensive after the peak (turnover stays near 5) or whether automation lets sales grow into it (turnover drifts back toward 6).

“For f y '27, we expect capital expenditure levels to be approximately three and a half percent of sales. We're hitting the peak of annual spending levels on supply chain automation and store remodels.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2026Q4_earnings_call_2026-02-19.txt, guidance
“We now expect slightly higher CapEx for the year at approximately 4% of annual net sales. Even with this increase, we expect to generate double-digit growth in free cash flow this year.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, guidance
“For the fiscal year ending January 31, 2027 ("fiscal 2027"), we project capital expenditures will be approximately $25 billion to $27 billion, with a focus on technology, supply chain and customer-facing initiatives.”10-K, 10k/WMT_10-K_FY2026_filed2026-03-13.htm, Liquidity and Capital Resources
“Our strategy includes allocating the majority of our capital to higher-return areas focused on automation such as eCommerce, supply chain and store and club investments.”10-K, 10k/WMT_10-K_FY2026_filed2026-03-13.htm, Capital Allocation

Why the suggestion sits where it does. A year-1 turnover of 5.2 makes implied FY2027 capex about $29.5 billion, roughly 4 percent of sales, matching the August guidance. Year 2 at 5.0 keeps capex near 3.8 percent for the second peak year; after that capex falls to about 3 percent of sales, below the peak but above the 3.2 percent of FY2024 relative to a smaller base.

Depreciation / prior net PP&E

History 2024 11.8%, 2025 11.7%, 2026 11.8%  ·  Suggested 11.8%, 11.8%, 11.8%, 11.8%, 11.8%, then 11.8% forever  ·  Moving it 1pp each way moves the value 12.8%

Depreciation and amortization over the prior year's net PP&E. It was 11.8, 11.7 and 11.8 percent in FY2024 to FY2026, very stable. The D&A line includes about $0.9 billion of finance lease amortization and some amortization of acquired intangibles (VIZIO, Flipkart), neither of which is depreciation of the PP&E in the denominator, so the rate is overstated by roughly 0.7 points. Useful lives are 3 to 40 years for buildings and 2 to 30 for fixtures and equipment. A rising share of short-lived automation and technology equipment would push the rate up over time; management already names higher depreciation as an SG&A headwind. Cross-check: realized capex from the PP&E roll-forward is $21.9, $22.2 and $30.3 billion for FY2024 to FY2026 against cash capex of $20.6, $23.8 and $26.6 billion; the FY2026 gap reflects finance-lease and other non-cash additions.

“One of the bigger drivers was depreciation. Depreciation is related to the CapEx that we've had has really been around supply chain automation and addressing speed.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, Q&A
“More than offsetting these benefits were higher depreciation related to CapEx and increased self-insurance cost.”John David Rainey, CFO, transcripts/WMT_FY2027Q2_earnings_call_2026-08-20.txt, prepared remarks

Why the suggestion sits where it does. Held at the three-year level of 11.8 percent. The mix shift toward automation argues for a slightly higher rate and the finance lease amortization in the numerator argues for a lower one; the two roughly offset.

Accounts payable

History 2023 8.8%, 2024 8.8%, 2025 8.6%, 2026 8.8%  ·  Suggested 8.8%, 8.8%, 8.8%, 8.8%, 8.8%, then 8.8% forever

Accounts payable over sales: 8.79, 8.77, 8.61 and 8.84 percent for FY2023 to FY2026, very stable. Payables nearly fund inventory (8.25 percent), which is the core of Walmart's working-capital model. Cash tax rate on taxable income

History 2022 25.4%, 2023 33.6%, 2024 25.5%, 2025 23.4%, 2026 24.4%  ·  Suggested 24.0%, 24.0%, 24.0%, 24.0%, 24.0%, then 24.0% forever  ·  Moving it 1pp each way moves the value 3.9%

The marginal cash rate on EBIT: 21 percent federal plus state and a mix of foreign rates. The effective rate was 24.4, 23.4 and 25.5 percent in FY2026, FY2025 and FY2024 per the 10-K, moved by non-deductible items (the PhonePe charge in FY2026) and the tax on investment gains and losses. Cash taxes paid were $5.4 billion in FY2026 on $29.5 billion of pretax income. The FY2023 ratio of 33.6 percent is distorted by non-deductible opioid settlements and investment losses. There is no usable federal NOL (see the opening NOL judgment), so the marginal and effective rates coincide in the forecast.

“Our effective income tax rate was 24.4%, 23.4%, and 25.5% for fiscal 2026, 2025 and 2024, respectively.”10-K, 10k/WMT_10-K_FY2026_filed2026-03-13.htm, Item 7 MD&A

Why the suggestion sits where it does. 24 percent, the centre of the last three effective rates once one-time items are set aside.

    Cost of capital

    A placeholder of 10 percent, not an estimate for this company. The true WACC should be calculated (cost of equity from CAPM, after-tax cost of debt, market-value weights) and entered on the Baseline tab or in the workbook's Inputs sheet.

    The model

    Sales grow at g. EBITDA is a margin on sales. Net PP&E at the end of year t is next year's sales divided by the sales-to-PP&E turnover — the one line that leads sales by a year, because capacity is built ahead of the volume it serves. Depreciation is a rate on prior-year net PP&E, and capex is the plug that reconciles the two; in steady state it lands at (g + d) times prior net PP&E. Every other operating asset and liability is a ratio to the same year's sales. Free cash flow is EBITDA less cash taxes on EBIT, less capex, less the change in working capital. Taxes are unlevered; the interest shield lives in the discount rate. A running NOL carryforward shelters the explicit years; anything still alive after year 6 is valued separately and appears in the bridge, so the perpetuity is taxed at the full rate and stays a genuine steady state.
